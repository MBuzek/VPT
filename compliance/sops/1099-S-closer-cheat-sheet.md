# 1099-S Cheat Sheet — For Closers

**Form 1099-S = "Proceeds From Real Estate Transactions."** As the settlement agent, **VPT is the party responsible for filing it.** When in doubt, **report** — under-reporting is the penalty risk, over-reporting is not.

> Golden rule: **Every seller/transferor gets a 1099-S UNLESS a specific exemption applies AND (for a primary residence) we hold a signed certification.**

---

## STEP 1 — Is it a reportable sale?
Report the sale or exchange (for money, debt, property, or services) of any ownership interest in:
- Land (improved or unimproved)
- Any permanent structure — residential, commercial, or industrial
- A condominium unit + its fixtures/common elements
- Stock in a cooperative housing corporation
- A non-contingent interest in standing timber

If yes → go to Step 2.

## STEP 2 — Does an EXEMPTION apply? (No 1099-S if ANY is true)
| # | Exemption | What to get / verify |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Primary residence ≤ $250K single / ≤ $500K married** and full gain excludable | **Seller must sign the 1099-S Certification (Assurances) form.** No signed form → REPORT. |
| 2 | **Seller is a corporation** or a government unit | Confirm entity type on the deed/settlement statement |
| 3 | **Total consideration < $600** | Check the sale price |
| 4 | **Not a sale/exchange** — gift, or a refinance / non-transfer | Confirm no change of ownership |
| 5 | **Property is outside the U.S.** | Confirm property location |
| 6 | **Volume transferor / IRS-exempt recipient** | Get their exempt certification |

No exemption applies → **REPORT** (Step 3).

## STEP 3 — Report it
- **Collect the seller's TIN/SSN** (W-9 or substitute) at or before closing — required.
- **Report GROSS proceeds** (total cash + notes + liabilities assumed), **not** net to seller.
- **One 1099-S per transferor.** Multiple sellers → get an allocation; without one, report the full amount to each.
- Enter closing date and property address.

---

## The primary-residence certification (the #1 exemption in practice)
1. If the sale looks like a primary residence at/under the threshold, have the **seller complete and sign the 1099-S Certification** ("Certification for No Information Reporting on the Sale/Exchange of a Principal Residence").
2. **The seller — not the closer — makes the certifications.** All must be "true."
3. **Retain the signed certification for 4 years.** No signed form on file = we report, no exceptions.

## Deadlines (calendar year after the sale)
- **Furnish statement to seller: by Feb 15**
- **File with IRS: Feb 28 (paper) / Mar 31 (e-file)** — e-file required at 10+ total information returns

## Do / Don't
- ✅ Get the TIN and (if applicable) the signed certification **before** funds disburse — chasing it after closing is painful.
- ✅ When the file is ambiguous, **report** and move on.
- ❌ Don't rely on a verbal "it's my primary residence" — no signed certification, no exemption.
- ❌ Don't net the proceeds or split one seller across forms.
- ❓ Entity sellers, 1031 exchanges, seller-financing, multi-parcel, or foreign sellers → **flag to Funding/Compliance** before deciding.
